# Freedom! — Short Adventure Reference

This is the table's simplified, kid-friendly version of *Freedom!* for Dark Sun, expanded
from a one-shot into a short 3-session mini-campaign. It is not a full conversion of the
original adventure. Use the original boxed set only as visual and tonal support when the DM
wants maps, handouts, or art at the table.

## Intended Use

- **Format:** 3-session mini-campaign. Session 1 was the escape from Tyr; Sessions 2 and 3
  are designed for 4-6 hours each.
- **Audience:** 2 adults and 2 children around age 10.
- **Rules:** Simplified D&D 5e with Dark Sun flavor and classes.
- **Priority:** Momentum, dramatic choices, and survival pressure over strict rules detail.
- **Dice:** Players roll for their characters. The DM/agent may roll only for NPCs or when asked.

## Core Premise

The heroes begin as gladiator slaves in Tyr during the chaos surrounding King Kalak's death.
They survive the arena, escape into the city, join a merchant caravan, and flee east into
the Tablelands. Their uncertain ally is Zareth, a human defiler whose secrets draw templar
pursuit toward everyone around him. The expanded version follows the group through the first
hard days of freedom: pursuit, thirst, betrayal, a dangerous refuge, and the decision of what
kind of free people they will become.

## Player Characters

- **Sszek:** Thri-Kreen fighter/hunter, desert survivor, sand-colored, fast and alert.
- **Korvak:** Half-Giant barbarian/gladiator, huge, direct, powerful, protective of the weak.
- **Drak:** Mul fighter/gladiator, tough, quiet, efficient, hard to kill.
- **Zareth:** DM-controlled human defiler, useful but dangerous, wanted by templars.

## Campaign Length

The adventure now works best as:

- **Session 1:** Arena, Tyr, caravan escape, first desert fight. Already played.
- **Session 2:** Pursuit across the Tablelands, caravan crisis, Zareth's secret, choice of refuge.
- **Session 3:** Dangerous refuge or mountain pass, final templar pressure, decision about Zareth
  and the freed slaves.

Do not expand into a full city-state campaign unless the table asks for it. The target ending
is not "the party fixes Tyr." It is "the party earns a fragile but real chance at freedom."

## Structure

### Act 1 — The Arena

Goal: escape the Grand Arena of Tyr during the confusion of Kalak's fall.

Expected beats:

- Open with heat, blood, crowd noise, and the feeling of being trapped.
- Arena beast attacks, with guards and gates creating urgency.
- Zareth reveals usefulness by casting a spell at the right moment.
- Give players clear, bold options: fight, leap, charge, distract, grab weapons, reach the gate.
- End once the party breaks through into Tyr's streets.

Already played:

- Arena creature defeated.
- Zareth cast *Sleep* on gate guards.
- Sszek attacked guards and leapt over them.
- Korvak and Drak charged the right gate.
- The party escaped into the Market District.

### Act 2 — The City

Goal: get out of Tyr before templars restore order.

Expected beats:

- Streets are chaotic: slaves running, merchants panicking, templars shouting orders.
- Present 2-3 useful opportunities, not a full city crawl:
  - Steal water or supplies.
  - Rally other escaped slaves.
  - Find transport or passage.
  - Use Zareth's magic to delay pursuit.
- Keep the scene short. Once the party has a plausible escape route, move to the gate.

Already played:

- Korvak stole a water wagon.
- Sszek recruited about six escaped slaves.
- Zareth cast *Fog Cloud* to confuse pursuing templars.
- Sszek negotiated passage with an eastbound merchant caravan by offering water and protection.

### Act 3 — The Desert Road

Goal: survive the Tablelands long enough to reach a believable point of freedom.

Expected beats:

- The desert should feel worse than the city: heat, thirst, no cover, predators, and pursuit.
- Use one major danger at a time. Good options:
  - Sand worm or desert predator.
  - Templar scouts or riders.
  - Caravan betrayal or panic.
  - Harsh terrain choice: fast open road vs dangerous hiding route.
- Force a choice about Zareth: protect him, abandon him, hand him over, or demand the truth.
- End with either escape into the Ringing Mountains/Tablelands or a final confrontation with pursuers.

Already played:

- Sand worm attacked the caravan.
- Hidden archers opened fire from rocky crags.
- Sszek damaged the worm with javelins; wooden weapons broke against its hide.
- Korvak killed the worm with a massive blow.
- Drak and the others fought off the ambushers.
- Zareth admitted templars are pursuing because of him.
- The party chose to keep Zareth after Sszek intimidated him and convinced the caravan leader.

## Expanded Session Plan

### Session 2 — Dust Behind Us

Goal: turn the escape into a survival story and make the party choose how much risk they will
accept for Zareth, the caravan, and the freed slaves.

Start:

- Open on the desert road at dusk.
- A column of dust behind the caravan reveals that mounted pursuers are closing.
- The road splits:
  - **Main road:** Faster, safer for wagons, visible to riders.
  - **Black-stone gulch:** Slower, dangerous terrain, better chance to hide or ambush.

Core scenes:

1. **The Road Choice**
   - Let the players debate openly.
   - The caravan leader argues for speed and protecting the cargo.
   - Zareth argues for the gulch, but refuses to explain how he knows it.
   - The freed slaves look to Sszek; they will follow his lead unless terrified.

2. **Pursuit Clock**
   - Track pressure in simple stages instead of exact time:
     - Stage 1: Dust on the horizon.
     - Stage 2: Riders visible.
     - Stage 3: Arrows or psionic warning shots.
     - Stage 4: Templars reach the caravan.
   - Good player plans reduce the clock by 1 stage or turn it into an ambush in their favor.
   - Loud delays, broken wagons, or failed checks advance the clock.

3. **Caravan Crisis**
   - Pick one complication based on the road chosen:
     - A wagon wheel cracks in the gulch.
     - A water barrel leaks on the main road.
     - One freed slave panics and runs.
     - The caravan leader tries to quietly negotiate with the pursuers.
   - Keep the problem physical and visible so the kids can act: lift, block, hide, shout, scout,
     threaten, repair, carry water, or protect someone.

4. **Zareth Pressed**
   - Zareth admits more under pressure:
     - He was in or near the Golden Tower when Kalak died.
     - He saw templars fighting each other over something.
     - He stole or copied a map fragment before fleeing.
   - He claims the map points to a hidden cistern or old refuge near the Ringing Mountains.
   - He does not admit whether the map is his only secret.

5. **Session 2 Climax**
   - Run one strong action sequence:
     - **Gulch ambush:** The party drops stones, blocks riders, or attacks from above.
     - **Road chase:** The party protects wagons while riders close in.
     - **Decoy plan:** The party sends the caravan one way while the heroes draw pursuit another.
   - Use 2-3 rounds of meaningful action. End when the tactical question is answered.

Session 2 ending states:

- **Strong success:** The pursuers are delayed badly. The caravan reaches a night camp near
  black rocks or foothills.
- **Mixed success:** The party escapes, but loses water, a wagon, or one caravan ally.
- **Failure with momentum:** The templars capture the caravan leader or a freed slave, forcing
  a rescue or hard choice in Session 3.

### Session 3 — The Price of Freedom

Goal: resolve the pursuit and decide the future of the party, Zareth, and the freed slaves.

Start:

- Begin at the edge of the Ringing Mountains, an old ruin, or a hidden cistern revealed by
  Zareth's map fragment.
- The location offers safety, but only if the party can claim it before the pursuers arrive
  or before its current danger overwhelms them.

Pick one main location:

- **Hidden Cistern:** Ancient stone basin under cracked ground. Water is real, but the entrance
  is narrow and unstable. Perfect for protecting refugees.
- **Old Watchpost:** Ruined tower from a forgotten age. Good vantage point, haunted by old traps
  or territorial creatures.
- **Slave Tribe Camp:** A wary band of escaped slaves already lives nearby. They may help, but
  they distrust Zareth and demand proof the party will not bring templars down on them.

Core scenes:

1. **Claim the Refuge**
   - Give the party a practical obstacle:
     - Clear rubble.
     - Drive off beasts.
     - Find hidden water.
     - Convince frightened survivors.
   - Make the kids' characters feel central: Korvak lifts or breaks, Sszek scouts or leads,
     Drak holds the line.

2. **The Truth of the Map**
   - Zareth's map does point to safety, but it also marks an older arcane site.
   - The templars want him because he knows which templar faction took something from the Golden Tower.
   - Keep the larger lore optional. The important table-facing truth is: Zareth can help the
     refugees survive, but keeping him means future danger.

3. **Final Pursuit**
   - The templars arrive with one clear leader, not a huge army.
   - Give the leader a simple offer:
     - "Give us Zareth and the slaves may walk."
     - "Give us the spellbook and we leave the caravan."
     - "Return to Tyr and be spared the desert."
   - The offer should be tempting enough to create table discussion, but cruel enough that the
     heroic answer is clear.

4. **Final Action**
   - Choose the finale that matches player plans:
     - Defend the cistern entrance.
     - Collapse a pass behind the caravan.
     - Duel the templar leader while others protect the freed slaves.
     - Use Zareth's dangerous magic at a visible cost to the land.
   - Let clever preparation matter more than raw damage.

5. **Freedom Choice**
   - After the danger breaks, ask what the party does next:
     - Stay and found a tiny free camp.
     - Travel with the caravan deeper into the Tablelands.
     - Send the freed slaves to safety and hunt Zareth's enemies.
     - Leave Zareth behind, keep him as an ally, or take his spellbook.

Session 3 ending states:

- **Heroic:** The refugees survive and the party becomes the seed of a small free band.
- **Bittersweet:** They escape, but with loss: water, followers, trust, or Zareth.
- **Dark Sun hook:** They win freedom, but Zareth's map points to something larger that can be
  used in a future campaign.

## Current State

- **Location:** Desert road east of Tyr, travelling with the merchant caravan.
- **Direction:** East toward the Tablelands and eventually the Ringing Mountains.
- **Party condition:** Drak and Korvak are hurt; Sszek and Zareth are stable.
- **Allies:** Merchant caravan, about six freed slaves loyal mainly to Sszek.
- **Threat:** Tyr templars know which road the caravan took and are closing in.
- **Unresolved questions:**
  - What exactly did Zareth see or steal in Tyr?
  - Will the caravan leader protect the party or sell them out?
  - Can the freed slaves survive the desert?

## Zareth's Secret

Use only as much as needed at the table.

Zareth witnessed something in the Golden Tower during the night Kalak died. He carries an
encrypted spellbook with a map fragment or clue that templars, defilers, and possibly the
Veiled Alliance would all want. He is not purely trustworthy: he will help the party when
their interests align, but he may sacrifice others to avoid capture.

For a short ending, the exact secret does not need a full explanation. It is enough that:

- The templars want him alive.
- He knows a route or refuge that can get the party away from Tyr.
- Keeping him creates danger but also gives the party a path forward.

## Recommended Next Scene

The caravan sees dust behind them: riders are catching up.

Offer a clear choice:

- **Main road:** Faster, safer for wagons, but visible and vulnerable to mounted pursuit.
- **Black-stone gulch:** Slower and risky, but easier to hide, ambush, or lose pursuers.

Possible outcomes:

- If they take the main road, run a chase or final stand with templar riders.
- If they take the gulch, run a tense ambush scene where the party can turn terrain against the pursuers.
- If they split or create a decoy, reward the plan and keep the final conflict smaller.

For the expanded campaign, do not resolve all pursuit in this one scene. Let this scene decide
the shape of Session 2: fast chase, terrain ambush, or caravan survival problem.

## Recurring Pressure Tools

Use these lightly when pacing slows:

- **Heat:** Exhaustion risk, mirages, metal too hot to touch, desperate need for shade.
- **Water:** Leaking barrels, ration arguments, a chance to trade safety for supplies.
- **Fear:** Freed slaves may scatter unless reassured or given clear orders.
- **Greed:** The caravan leader protects profit first unless the party earns his loyalty.
- **Zareth:** Helpful spell, suspicious silence, partial truth, dangerous offer.
- **Templars:** Distant dust, signal horns, arrows from ridges, an offer to betray Zareth.

## Simple Encounter Menu

Use one per major scene, not all of them.

- **Templar riders:** 3-5 mounted pursuers. Dangerous if they reach open ground, weaker in tight
  terrain.
- **Psionic tracker:** One quiet enemy who can sense fear or arcane residue. Better as pressure
  than as a straight fight.
- **Crodlu stampede:** Desert mounts panic and threaten wagons or refugees.
- **Silt sink:** Ground collapses like quicksand; rescue scene, not combat.
- **Broken water wagon:** Forces a choice between speed, supplies, and helping the weak.
- **Refugee panic:** A freed slave runs, hides, or begs to surrender.
- **Defiling backlash:** Zareth can solve a problem with magic, but plants wither and animals
  scream around him.

## Ending Options

### Quick Heroic Ending

The party uses terrain, courage, and Zareth's last spell to break the pursuit. The caravan
continues east. The freed slaves look to Sszek and the others as the first leaders of a new
free band in the desert.

### Bittersweet Dark Sun Ending

The party escapes, but the caravan loses wagons, water, or followers. Freedom is real, but
survival is still uncertain. Zareth offers a dangerous lead toward shelter in the Ringing
Mountains.

### Zareth Twist Ending

Zareth helps save the group, then reveals he must leave before his enemies find him again.
He offers the party a choice: keep his secret and gain a future ally, or take his spellbook
and make themselves targets of larger forces.

## Tone Reminders

- Keep choices concrete and urgent.
- Let kids solve problems with big, visible actions.
- Reward creative survival ideas.
- Avoid long lore dumps. Reveal Athas through danger, scarcity, and NPC behavior.
- Keep combat punchy. Two or three meaningful rounds are usually enough.
- Make victory feel earned, but do not make the ending hopeless.
